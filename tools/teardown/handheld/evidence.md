# Handheld console: sources and inventions

The drawing shows a concept console, not any real product. Nothing here is
measured from, or traced off, an existing device.

## Taken from the category

These are the general conventions of current handhelds, not the trade dress
of any one of them:

- A landscape slab with rounded ends, the screen centred, and controls in
  both grips.
- A D-pad and a stick on the left, four face buttons and a stick on the
  right, and two small menu keys under the screen.
- A two-part shell that splits at a parting line. The front carries the
  cover glass; the back is a shallow tub with screw bosses that hold the
  board.
- Inside, from the back shell up: a pouch battery in a ribbed bay held by
  adhesive strips with pull tabs, the mainboard, a small blower fan, the
  display module, and a game card slot at the far edge.
- Board conventions: an SoC with decoupling capacitors, memory and storage on
  short buses, a PMIC with inductors, a shielded radio with a meander antenna,
  and plated mounting holes. Traces run straight and at 45°.

## Invented

- Every dimension. The body is 176 × 80 × 14 mm, chosen so the layout reads
  well at home page size.
- All markings: "PSRAY", the model and part numbers, the chip names
  ("PSR X1"), the battery rating and the serial numbers.
- The game card, "TROPHY QUEST" by "PSRAY GAMES", with its pixel-art label.
  Real game titles and art were left out on purpose: they are other
  companies' trademarks and copyrighted work, on a public page.
- The face-button letters and colours, and the four colour schemes.
